02 / EDA — Pattern 1

Which customer groups prefer digital transactions?

Digital share = (UPI + Card transactions) ÷ all channel transactions, averaged per segment.

By age group

By occupation

Students & Salaried lead; Retirees lag
Finding: age and occupation are the dominant drivers of digital preference (84%→16% across age; 83%→25% across occupation). City/location is a weak driver by comparison — the 12 cities in this dataset span only 58%–64%, so geographic targeting is not worth prioritising over age/occupation segmentation.
03 / EDA — Pattern 2

Is ATM usage declining, and where?

Average monthly ATM transaction count per age group, Month 1 through Month 6.

Finding: ATM usage is actively declining in the 26–45 age bands (26–35: −31% Month1→Month6; 36–45: −15%) as these customers migrate to digital channels mid-stream. The 56+ segments show flat-to-rising ATM reliance (65+: +10%) — they are not migrating away from cash channels at all.

06 / EDA — Relationships

How the behaviour metrics relate

Pearson correlation across all engineered features. Blue = negative, red = positive, grey = no relationship.

Finding: Digital_Ratio and ATM_Count correlate at −0.69 — genuine channel substitution, not two independently growing behaviours. Deposits and Withdrawals correlate at 0.95, as expected for active transactors.
07–08 / KNN model

Build, evaluate & interpret the KNN classifier

Target: Digital_Preferring (UPI+Card count above the base median). Features deliberately exclude UPI_Count/Card_Count/Digital_Ratio to avoid leakage — only demographics, ATM behaviour and monetary activity are used, since a bank has those on file even for customers with thin digital history.

Interpretation: Age_Group is by far the strongest predictor (0.172), followed by Occupation (0.064) and Bill_Payments (0.063) — directly matching the EDA patterns above. ATM_Trend_Slope contributes only marginally once Age_Group is already in the model: its signal is largely redundant with age, not because the ATM decline pattern is false (Section 03 confirmed it independently), but because age alone already separates the same customers. City and raw Deposits/Withdrawals carry essentially no predictive signal.

Methodology. No public dataset matched this exact per-customer, 6-month schema, so a synthetic 1,500-customer dataset was generated with segment-conditioned behaviour (age/occupation-driven digital adoption, ATM trend decay, injected outliers) plus deliberate data-quality issues, then processed through 10 standalone, independently re-runnable scripts (01_generate_data.py through 10_model_interpretation.py) in D:\DS_Hack\scripts\. This dashboard renders the exact numbers those scripts printed on their last verified run — nothing here is re-derived or estimated separately.
